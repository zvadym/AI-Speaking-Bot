# Tone of Voice Guidelines

**Project:** AI Language School bots, mini-app, landing, and related product copy  
**Status:** Working guideline for content generation, UX copy, notifications, and prompt writing  
**Language:** English source document, intended to guide copywriting in all product languages

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how product text should sound across the project.

It is not a list of fixed phrases. It is a decision framework for writing tutor replies, onboarding copy, corrections, reminders, subscription text, and other short product strings in a way that:

- feels human, not synthetic
- supports practice, not performance
- stays learning-first, not entertainment-first
- remains clear across multiple product languages
- avoids generic “AI-ish” wording

---

## 2. Product and brand guardrails

The product helps users build a steady speaking habit in their target language through short text and voice practice inside Telegram.

That means the voice should feel:

- calm
- clear
- practical
- respectful
- compact
- teacher-like without sounding formal
- grounded in real language use

It should **not** feel like:

- a hype machine
- a generic AI chatbot
- a therapy app
- a motivational quote generator
- a gamified streak bully
- a grammar textbook
- a flirting assistant or parasocial companion

### Core brand stance

**Practice-first, not performance-first.**

### Core tone direction

**Calm teacher with human wording.**

This means:

- warm, but not sugary
- supportive, but not therapeutic
- direct, but not harsh
- brief, but not robotic
- encouraging, but not overpraising

---

## 3. The main writing rule

## Describe the next action, not a vague feeling.

Bad copy often describes an abstract mood:

- sound more natural
- keep it flowing
- make it smoother
- speak more confidently
- try again gently

Good copy describes a visible action:

- send one short voice message
- answer in 1-2 sentences
- use the past tense here
- change the preposition
- ask one follow-up question
- stop there if the reply is already clear

This is the best filter against LLM-sounding text in this product.

## 4. Voice principles

### 4.1 Write like a teacher, not like a model

Use everyday wording. Prefer the language a calm teacher would actually use in chat.

Do not write copy that sounds polished just to sound polished.

### 4.2 One line = one job

Each line should do one of these things:

1. tell the user what to do next
2. explain what changed or why
3. reduce pressure without becoming vague

If a line does none of these, it is probably filler.

### 4.3 Favor short concrete sentences

Prefer:

- Send one short message.
- Use the past tense here.
- This word needs an article.
- If you want, try again with voice.

Over:

- Please continue practicing in a way that feels natural and manageable for you.

### 4.4 Reduce pressure without adding fog

The product should lower pressure, but not by using abstract cushioning words.

Use:

- One short reply is enough.
- You can keep it simple.
- If you are busy, send one sentence.
- We can stop here and continue later.

Avoid:

- Take your time and let it flow naturally.
- Try to keep it comfortable.
- Approach this gently.
- Make it feel organic.

### 4.5 Support practice, not self-performance

The product is about reps, not impressing the bot.

Prefer language around:

- speaking
- replying
- trying again
- correcting
- noticing patterns
- repeating
- building a habit

Avoid language around:

- mastering instantly
- sounding impressive
- winning
- crushing it
- performing perfectly

### 4.6 Respect the learner’s pace

Copy should make practice feel doable, not heavy.

Prefer:

- 1-2 sentences is enough.
- A short voice note works well.
- We can continue later.
- Let’s fix one thing at a time.

Avoid:

- You must stay consistent.
- Do not break your streak.
- You need to practice more seriously.

### 4.7 Respect the other person in examples and coaching

When coaching conversation, leave room for normal social boundaries.

Prefer:

- keep the question short
- wait for the reply
- if the conversation ends, stop there

Avoid:

- push the conversation further
- keep the interaction going no matter what
- force a response

## 5. Language-shaping rules for all locales

This document is in English, but the rules should be applied by meaning, not by literal translation.

### 5.1 Optimize for local everyday speech

In every target language, choose the version that sounds like normal app or teacher language in that locale.

Do not preserve English sentence structure if it makes the local text sound translated.

### 5.2 Informality should follow local norms

Use the locally appropriate second-person form for a personal consumer learning product.

General default:

- prefer informal, direct address if that is normal for friendly educational UX in that language
- avoid slang unless a specific teacher persona clearly calls for it
- avoid formal distance unless the locale strongly expects it

### 5.3 Ban the pattern, not only the exact word

Every language has its own version of AI filler.

Do not only ban specific English words such as:

- gently
- naturally
- smoothly
- comfortably
- organically

Also ban their local equivalents when they serve the same function: vague emotional smoothing without clear behavioral meaning.

### 5.4 Translate intent, not surface wording

Example:

English source:

> Send one short voice message. I’ll correct one or two important things.

Good localized versions should preserve:

- clear action
- low pressure
- practical teaching value
- brief human wording

They do **not** need to preserve the exact syntax.

### 5.5 Keep the learning split clear

When the product uses both learner language and target language:

- UI, explanations, billing text, and reminders should usually be clearest in the base language
- practice turns, example answers, and suggested user replies should sound natural in the target language
- do not mix the two without a reason

## 6. Preferred voice profile

The default voice profile for product copy is:

### Calm teacher

It should sound:

- steady
- human
- brief
- useful
- non-dramatic
- emotionally clean

It should not sound:

- cute
- overly clever
- over-motivational
- over-empathetic
- clinical
- pushy

## 7. Writing guidance by use case

### 7.1 Tutor replies

Tutor replies should feel like real conversation plus useful guidance.

They should:

- answer the user first
- stay brief
- sound like a chat partner, not a script
- avoid repeating the user’s message back to them
- move the conversation forward naturally

They should not:

- greet on every turn
- summarize the transcript
- over-explain inside the conversational reply
- praise every message with generic lines like “Great job” or “Amazing”

### 7.2 Corrections and explanations

Corrections should name the concrete issue and the concrete fix.

Prefer:

- We need **the** here because this noun is specific.
- Use **go to work**, not **go to the work**.
- This verb needs past tense.

Avoid:

- This sounds more natural.
- This is better.
- This feels smoother.

### 7.3 Onboarding

Onboarding copy should reduce friction and get the user to the first rep quickly.

Prefer:

- Send one short voice message.
- Start with 1-2 sentences.
- Choose your level.

Avoid:

- Begin your language journey.
- Unlock your speaking potential.
- Express yourself with confidence.

### 7.4 Notifications and winbacks

Notifications should reopen the loop with low pressure.

Prefer:

- Ready for a quick practice? Send one short message.
- Want to continue our conversation? Write one sentence.

Avoid:

- We miss you.
- Your streak is waiting.
- Do not lose momentum now.

### 7.5 Subscription and billing copy

Billing text should be plain, calm, and factual.

Prefer:

- Manage your plan, usage, and billing.
- Voice usage this month.
- Turn off auto-renew.

Avoid:

- Unlock the full magic.
- Supercharge your fluency.
- Upgrade now before it’s too late.

### 7.6 Landing and marketing copy

Marketing can be warmer and more vivid than in-product copy, but it must still stay grounded.

Prefer:

- practice for real conversations
- short voice and text reps
- clear corrections
- steady speaking habit

Avoid:

- speak like a native fast
- transform your life
- perfect fluency overnight
- AI that feels just like a real human relationship

## 8. Word patterns to prefer

Prefer:

- practice
- short reply
- voice message
- correction
- explanation
- example
- try again
- clear
- simple
- useful
- steady
- natural speech
- everyday language

Use carefully:

- confidence
- progress
- streak
- natural

These words are fine when they point to something concrete, but weak when they become vague slogans.

---

## 9. Word patterns to avoid

Avoid when possible:

- gently help you sound more natural
- smooth and comfortable
- organic conversation
- unlock your potential
- level up your speaking
- perfect your fluency
- magical AI teacher
- crush your goals
- no-pressure vibe

These phrases usually signal generic AI copy, vague promises, or inflated tone.

---

## 10. Final test before shipping copy

Before approving a line, check:

1. Would a calm teacher actually say this?
2. Does it describe a clear action, fix, or outcome?
3. Is it short enough to feel natural in chat or UI?
4. Did we remove filler, vague cushioning, and hype?
5. Would this still sound good after translation?

If not, rewrite it in simpler, more concrete language.
